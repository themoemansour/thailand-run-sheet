09 Eating — and the pork problem

None of you eat pork, and Thai street food is built on it. Pork is the default protein in most of the famous street dishes, and worse, it hides in broths and garnishes that look nothing like pork. Here's what to avoid, what to eat instead, and the exact words to say.

The word is “moo” — หมู If a dish name contains moo, it is pork. That one word catches most of it off a menu board. Gai (ไก่) is chicken, goong (กุ้ง) shrimp, pla (ปลา) fish, nuea (เนื้อ) beef, talay (ทะเล) seafood. Learn those five and you can read half a street menu.
The broth is the trap This is the one that catches people. Most Thai noodle-soup broths are pork-bone based even when the meat on top is chicken. Ordering “chicken noodles” does not make the bowl pork-free. Ask nam soup moo mai? — is the broth pork? At a busy stall the honest answer is usually yes, and the right response is to eat elsewhere rather than argue.
Two more hidden ones Kak moo / nam man moo — crispy fried pork fat, scattered over noodle dishes as a default garnish without being mentioned. Say mai sai kak moo. And plenty of stalls fry in pork lard rather than oil, which no amount of ordering around fixes. If that matters, halal kitchens are the clean answer.

Famous dishes that are pork

All of these get recommended to tourists. All are out.

Moo ping
หมูปิ้ง · grilled pork skewers
The most common street snack in Thailand, on every corner with sticky rice. Sold from the same carts as chicken, so ask for gai yang instead.
Khao kha moo
ข้าวขาหมู · stewed pork leg on rice
The glistening brown hock under a glass case. A national obsession and completely off-limits.
Khao moo daeng
ข้าวหมูแดง · red pork on rice
Sweet red-edged slices over rice, usually with crispy pork belly alongside. Chinatown staple.
Moo krob
หมูกรอบ · crispy pork belly
Appears as an add-on to rice plates, stir-fries and noodles, often unannounced. Learn it by sight: cubes with a bubbled white crust.
Guay jub
ก็วยจั๊บ · peppery rolled-noodle soup
Yaowarat's signature late-night bowl. Pork offal and crispy pork in a pork broth. Triple no.
Leng saap
เล้งแซ่บ · spicy pork spine soup
Correcting myself — I flagged this as the thing to order at Jodd Fairs. It's pork spine. Skip it; the grilled seafood and salmon stalls there are excellent instead.
Moo krata
หมูกระทะ · Thai BBQ hotpot
The dome-grill all-you-can-eat places. The format is built on pork fat melting down the dome into the broth, so even the vegetables aren't clean.
Sai krok Isan
ไสกรอกอีสาน · fermented sausage
The short fat sour sausages on grills at every market in the country. Pork and rice.
Larb moo & nam tok moo
ลาบหมู · minced and grilled pork salads
Both have chicken and beef versions that are just as good. Ask for larb gai or nam tok nuea and you lose nothing at all.
Boat noodles
กฏวยเตีๆยวเรือ · kuay teow reua
Even the beef version is usually thickened with pork blood. Famous, tiny bowls, not worth the interrogation.
Kanom jeeb & wontons
dumplings
Default filling is pork unless the stall says otherwise. Shrimp versions exist — confirm before ordering.

Eat these instead

Naturally pork-free, and not one of them a consolation prize.

Massaman curry
แกงมัสมั่น · gaeng massaman
Start here. A Muslim-origin curry — the name comes from “Mussulman” — so it's chicken or beef by tradition, never pork. Cardamom, cinnamon, peanuts, potato. Regularly voted one of the best dishes in the world, and on every menu in the country.
Khao mok gai
ข้าวหมกไก่ · Thai chicken biryani
Thailand's Muslim biryani — turmeric rice, chicken, green dipping sauce. Where you find it, the kitchen is halal by definition. Common in Phuket and around Bangkok's mosque neighbourhoods.
Pad kra pao gai
ผัดกะเพราไก่ · chicken holy basil
The national everyday plate. Say gai clearly, because moo is the default. Add kai dao for a crispy fried egg on top. Under ฿70 anywhere.
Gai yang & som tam
ไก่ย่าง · grilled chicken + papaya salad
The Isaan combination, with sticky rice. Order som tam thai — peanuts and dried shrimp. Avoid som tam pu pla ra, which is fermented fish and raw crab, and will end your evening.
Tom yum goong
ต้มยำกุ้ง
Shrimp, lemongrass, galangal, lime. The broth is shrimp-based, so it's one of very few soups with no pork question at all.
Tom kha gai
ต้มข่าไก่
Coconut and galangal chicken soup. Mild, which matters when everything else on the table is trying to hurt you.
Pla pao
ปลาเผา · salt-crusted grilled fish
Whole fish packed in salt and lemongrass over charcoal, cracked open at the table. The best thing at any Thai night market, and always pork-free.
Goong ob woonsen
กุ้งอบวุ้นเส้น
Prawns baked in a clay pot over glass noodles with ginger and pepper. Order it at any seafood place — it's the dish to get five people around.
Hoy tod
หอยทอด · crispy mussel omelette
Mussels or oysters in a crackling batter with bean sprouts. Street-cart classic with no pork anywhere near it.
Khao soi gai
ข้าวซอยไก่
Northern coconut curry noodles with chicken and crispy noodles on top. The broth is coconut-curry rather than pork stock, so this is the safe noodle soup.
Gai satay & roti
สะเตะะไก่ · โรตี
Satay is often pork — specify gai. Roti, the fried flatbread with condensed milk and banana, is a Thai-Muslim street dessert and it's everywhere in Phuket.
Mango sticky rice
ข้าวเหนียวมะม่วง
Non-negotiable. Best from market carts where they slice the mango in front of you. Roughly ฿60–100.

The catch-all: ahan jay (อาหารเจ) means Buddhist vegan. Any stall flying a yellow-and-red เจ flag has no meat of any kind in the kitchen, which sidesteps the broth question entirely when you can't get a straight answer.

Where to eat — Pattaya

Local and cheap, and not on Walking Street.

Thepprasit runs exactly across your three nights. Go for the grilled seafood rows and the gai yang carts; walk past the sausage grills. Bali Hai is the move after a Koh Larn day — buy fish by the kilo and they cook it for you.

Where to eat — Phuket

By far the easiest place on this trip for you.

Rawai seafood marketBest value
Chillva MarketTue–Sat
Kata Night MarketCheap
Old Town, Thalang RdAfternoon

Phuket has a large Muslim population — roughly a third of the island — so halal kitchens are genuinely everywhere, heaviest in the north and around Old Town. Look for the green halal mark. Rawai is the play: buy fish, crab or prawns off the ice by the kilo, hand them to a restaurant across the road, and they cook it for a small fee.

Where to eat — Bangkok

One honest warning in here.

YaowaratPork-heavy

Yaowarat needs a caveat. I put it on the must-do list and it stays — but Chinatown is the most pork-dense eating in Thailand and several of its famous stalls are out for you. Go for the grilled seafood, the oyster omelettes and the fruit; skip the noodle bowls. Sukhumvit Soi 3 is the answer on other nights — the Arab quarter, entirely halal, minutes from Nana BTS and from where you're staying.
