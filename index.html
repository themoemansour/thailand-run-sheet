04 Read this part twice

Four things changed in Thailand recently. Three carry fines or jail. One is good news.

Vapes are fully illegal. This is the big one. Banned since 2014, but a crackdown ordered in 2025 turned that from a paper rule into an enforced one — 120,000+ devices seized and 690 arrests in a single week. Tourists face ฿20,000–30,000 fines, confiscation and jail; bringing one through customs carries up to ten years under the Customs Act. There is no nicotine-free exception, no personal-use allowance, and it covers IQOS and every heat-not-burn device. Checks happen in arrivals. Patong, Walking Street and Sukhumvit are the most policed streets in the country — which is to say, exactly where you'll be. Leave them at home. All of them. Check jacket pockets.
Weed is not legal any more Re-criminalised June 25, 2025. Medical-only, requiring a Thai PT 33 prescription from a Thai practitioner, valid 30 days or less. Your card from home means nothing. Buying without a prescription or consuming in public runs to ฿25,000 and up to three months inside. Roughly 7,300 of 18,400 shops have closed. The ones still open are not evidence it's fine.
Good news: the afternoon booze ban is gone The old 14:00–17:00 blackout, a 1972 rule meant to stop civil servants drinking at lunch, was scrapped permanently on May 29, 2026. Licensed venues sell continuously 11:00 to midnight, and entertainment venues run to their own licence hours, so clubs go later. No Buddhist holiday bans fall in your window.
Your US licence does not let you ride a scooter Thailand requires a motorcycle licence from home plus an IDP carrying the Category A motorcycle endorsement. Car-only IDP doesn't count; a home licence alone gets rejected. Fines ฿500–2,000, and Patong has some of the most persistent checkpoints in the country. The fine isn't the problem — travel insurance is void in any accident where you weren't legally licensed. Use Grab and Bolt.

09 Eating — and the pork problem

None of you eat pork, and Thai street food is built on it. Pork is the default protein in most of the famous street dishes, and worse, it hides in broths and garnishes that look nothing like pork. Here's what to avoid, what to eat instead, and the exact words to say.

The word is “moo” — หมู If a dish name contains moo, it is pork. That one word catches most of it off a menu board. Gai (ไก่) is chicken, goong (กุ้ง) shrimp, pla (ปลา) fish, nuea (เนื้อ) beef, talay (ทะเล) seafood. Learn those five and you can read half a street menu.
The broth is the trap This is the one that catches people. Most Thai noodle-soup broths are pork-bone based even when the meat on top is chicken. Ordering “chicken noodles” does not make the bowl pork-free. Ask nam soup moo mai? — is the broth pork? At a busy stall the honest answer is usually yes, and the right response is to eat elsewhere rather than argue.
Two more hidden ones Kak moo / nam man moo — crispy fried pork fat, scattered over noodle dishes as a default garnish without being mentioned. Say mai sai kak moo. And plenty of stalls fry in pork lard rather than oil, which no amount of ordering around fixes. If that matters, halal kitchens are the clean answer.

Eat these instead

Naturally pork-free, and not one of them a consolation prize.

Massaman curry

Start here. A Muslim-origin curry — the name comes from “Mussulman” — so it's chicken or beef by tradition, never pork. Cardamom, cinnamon, peanuts, potato. Regularly voted one of the best dishes in the world, and on every menu in the country.
Khao mok gai

Thailand's Muslim biryani — turmeric rice, chicken, green dipping sauce. Where you find it, the kitchen is halal by definition. Common in Phuket and around Bangkok's mosque neighbourhoods.
Pad kra pao gai

Pla pao

Goong ob woonsen

Hoy tod

Khao soi gai

Northern coconut curry noodles with chicken and crispy noodles on top. The broth is coconut-curry rather than pork stock, so this is the safe noodle soup.

10 Health, and what Thai pharmacies will hand you

Thailand has excellent private hospitals and pharmacies that dispense a lot of what needs a doctor at home. Mostly that's a good thing, with two or three sharp edges.

Before you fly

Travel clinic about four weeks out. You have roughly six.

Hepatitis AStrongly advised
TyphoidAdvised
Tetanus / diphtheriaBoost if due
Hepatitis BAdvised
Rabies, pre-exposureOptional
Malaria tabletsNot needed

Skip the malaria pills. Bangkok, Pattaya, Phuket and Phi Phi carry no meaningful malaria risk, so you'd be taking side effects for nothing. Hep A is the one that actually matters given how much street food you're about to eat.

What will actually get you

Ordered by likelihood, not drama.

Traveller's diarrhoeaVery likely
Sunburn and heatLikely
SeasicknessSeveral boat days
DengueReal risk
Animal biteSerious if it happens

Dengue matters more than people assume. It's mosquito-borne and the Aedes mosquito bites in daylight, so evening repellent isn't enough. There's no practical traveller vaccine. If anyone runs a high fever, paracetamol only — no ibuprofen, no aspirin, because both raise bleeding risk if it turns out to be dengue. DEET 30% or picaridin, long sleeves at dusk.

Monkey Beach, specifically

This is on your Phi Phi day, Tue Nov 17.

The monkeys bite and they steal. They go for bags, phones, water bottles and anything that rustles. Don't feed them, don't hold food, don't crowd them for a photo, and take your sunglasses off — they grab.

Any bite or scratch, including one that barely breaks skin: wash it with soap under running water for a full fifteen minutes, then get to a hospital the same day for rabies shots. Thailand has rabies and it is effectively always fatal once symptoms appear, so this is not a wait-and-see. Post-exposure treatment is widely available and it works. Same rule for the stray dogs, which are everywhere.

Over the counter there, prescription at home

Boots and Watsons on every other corner. Pharmacists speak decent English and will sort minor things across the counter.

Antibiotics — amoxicillin, azithromycin, ciproOTC
Fluconazole and antifungalsOTC

Two warnings that come with that. Self-prescribing antibiotics is genuinely a bad idea — wrong drug, wrong dose, and it can mask something that needs a doctor. Treat them as a backup when you can't reach a clinic, not a first move. And buy from Boots, Watsons or a hospital pharmacy, never a market stall: counterfeit medication exists here.

PEP for HIV is hospital-only, not a pharmacy item, and has a hard 72-hour window that gets less effective every hour inside it. Bumrungrad or Samitivej in Bangkok, Bangkok Hospital in Phuket and Pattaya.

Private hospitals here are excellent and will want payment or a guarantee up front. Save the policy number in your phone and on paper, because the phone is the thing most likely to be gone.

The fee is per withdrawal, not per baht — take the maximum every time, not a little and often. Always choose to be billed in Thai baht; the home-currency prompt is dynamic currency conversion and the markup eats any card benefit you have.

Better than ATMs: bring clean USD notes and change them at SuperRich or Vasu in Bangkok. Their margins beat both the airport counters and the machines. Never change more than the van fare at the airport.

Etiquette that actually matters

Four things. The rest you'll pick up.

Feet are the lowest thing, heads the highest. Don't point your feet at people or at a Buddha image, don't step over someone sitting down, and don't touch an adult's head.

Shoes off at temples, homes, and plenty of small shops and guesthouses. If there's a pile by the door, add to it.

Never joke about the monarchy. Thailand's lèse-majesté law is real, carries years in prison, and foreigners have been prosecuted under it. Don't test it, including online while you're in the country.

Keep your temper. Losing it in public loses you the argument in Thai terms — the person who stays calm is right. It's also the single most effective tool against a driver trying it on.

12 Scams, ranked by how often they land

1. “The Grand Palace is closed today” Someone official-looking outside says it's shut for a ceremony. It is not — it's open 08:30–15:30 daily. He has a tuk-tuk friend who'll take you to a better temple, then a gem shop, then a tailor. Walk past and go to the actual gate.
2. The twenty-baht tuk-tuk A cross-town ride for ฿20, or free, if you'll just stop at one shop. The driver earns ฿500–2,000 in commission per tourist delivered. You'll lose an hour and nothing you buy is worth what you pay.
3. The ping-pong show Touts with laminated menus on Walking Street and Bangla. The advertised price is the door. Inside, drinks you didn't order appear, the bill is four figures, and a large man stands between you and the stairs. There is no version of this that ends well, and you will be offered it on your first night in Pattaya.
4. Jet ski damage You return it, the operator points at a scratch that was already there and demands ฿10,000–30,000. Endemic in Patong and Pattaya, both of which you're staying in. Film the whole machine before you touch it, operator in shot — or just don't.
5. The broken meter Bangkok driver refuses the meter and quotes triple. Get out. Grab and Bolt price it before you're in the car.
6. TDAC fee sites The arrival card is free at the official immigration site. Dozens of lookalikes charge $20–40 to file a form that takes five minutes.
